Designing credit card disputes out of the ombudsman queue

Credit card complaints to the RBI Ombudsman grew 20% in a year. From two years in customer care I know why people escalate: they can't see what is happening to their problem. This project turns the ombudsman data into a dispute-tracking product.

What the data shows

  • Complaints are rising overall. RBI's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme received 13.34 lakh complaints in FY25, up 13.55%.
  • Credit cards moved up the list. They became the second-largest category at 17.15% of complaints received by ombudsman offices, behind loans and advances at 29.25%.
  • Card complaints grow faster than the rest. Credit card complaints rose 20.04% to 50,811, well ahead of the 13.55% growth in all complaints.
  • Regulators act on the root causes. In October 2025 RBI fined one card issuer ₹31.80 lakh for not refunding credit balances left on cards after refunds and failed transactions.

Why customers escalate

In customer care I handled more than 500 conversations a month. Disputed charges and missing refunds followed the same pattern:

  1. The customer raises a dispute and gets a reference number but no timeline.
  2. They call again to ask for status and explain the problem from the start.
  3. Each contact adds frustration. After 30 days without a resolution they can go to the RBI Ombudsman.
The fix is visibility. Most "where is my refund?" calls ask for information the bank already has but doesn't show.

As a cardholder with a disputed charge, I want to see exactly where my dispute is and when it will move, so that I don't have to call and explain it again.

As an operations analyst, I want every dispute to carry its SLA date, so that I work the ones about to breach first.

Success metrics

  • North star: card complaints escalated to the ombudsman per 10,000 cards.
  • Input metrics: % of disputes resolved within SLA, repeat contacts per dispute, tracker views per dispute.
  • Guardrails: chargeback loss rate, time to first response, CSAT on resolved disputes.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Showing a timeline creates a promise. Dates must come from real SLAs, not hopeful estimates.
  • Operations teams may resist new visibility. Shared SLA dashboards make the tracker a tool for them too.
  • Automating refunds needs clean account data. Start with customers who have a verified linked account.

Prototype and test plan

I built the dispute tracker as a clickable prototype so it can be tested with real cardholders before anyone writes production code. It covers checking status, raising a dispute, a delayed step and sending a credit balance back to the bank.

Open the clickable prototype

The plan: 5 cardholders who raised a dispute in the last year, 5 tasks each, with time and success recorded. The design changes wherever two or more people fail the same task. The tasks are listed beside the prototype.
